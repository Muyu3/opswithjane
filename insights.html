Insights on operations, CRM systems and workflow automation

Short, practical notes on running a service business that does not depend on the founder.

Check whether it will work before you automate it

The most expensive automation is the one built on a process that was never sound. Before I build anything, I ask what problem it solves, who touches it, and what happens when something unusual comes through. If the answer is unclear, automating it only makes the confusion faster. Often the right first step is a simpler process, written down, with one tool doing one job. Then automate.

The gap between enquiry and onboarding is where clients get lost

For many service businesses, lead generation is not the weak point. The weak point is the stretch between someone showing interest and a client being properly onboarded. Forms live in one tool, bookings in another, contracts in a third, and follow-up depends on someone remembering. A single structured journey, with clear pipeline stages and reminders, closes that gap without adding headcount.

A business that only runs when you are in the room

If every task needs you, you do not have a business so much as a demanding job. The goal of good operations is not to remove the founder from the work they enjoy. It is to remove them from the work that only exists because nothing is documented, scheduled or owned. Start by listing the tasks you touched this week, then ask which ones a clear process and a reliable person could own.
